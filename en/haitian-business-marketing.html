Whether your customers are in Little Haiti, Flatbush, Montréal-Nord, Mattapan or Delmas, you have probably been offered marketing by people who had to look up where that was. We start from the other end.

What usually goes wrong

Haitian-owned businesses across the diaspora tend to have the same shape of problem, and it is rarely the one agencies try to sell against.

The business runs on bouch-a-zòrèy, and it works: up to a point

Word of mouth inside the community is genuinely powerful. Church on Sunday, the barber, the WhatsApp group, the cousin who knows somebody. Plenty of businesses have been built entirely on it and have never needed anything else.

The ceiling is that it only travels as far as the existing circle. The second-generation customer who searches in English, the neighbour who is not Haitian, the person who moved into the area last year: none of them are in that circle, and none of them will ever hear your name.

The presence is on Facebook, and only on Facebook

A Facebook page with 2,000 followers, posts in Kreyòl, and an active comment section is a real asset, often more real than the website of a competitor twice the size.

But it does not appear on a map when somebody searches. It cannot be found by anyone who is not already looking for you by name. And you do not own it: one policy change or one locked account and a decade of customer relationships is gone.

Language gets treated as an afterthought

Either everything is in Kreyòl, which quietly excludes the wider neighbourhood and the second generation, or everything is in English, which quietly tells the aunties this place is not really for them.

The answer is not to pick one. It is to be deliberate about which language does which job, and that requires somebody who can actually hear the difference.

What we do about it

The same 45-day foundation we build for everyone, with three things that are simply assumed rather than negotiated.

  • Kreyòl is a first language, not a translation pass. Copy is written in it, not converted into it. There is a difference and your customers can hear it immediately.
  • WhatsApp is the main channel. Not a form. Click-to-chat on every page, on the map listing and on a QR code by the register, because that is how people in this community actually make contact.
  • You get found by people outside the circle. A Google Business Profile done properly, so the neighbour who searches in English finds you too.
The goal is not to replace word of mouth. It is to stop word of mouth from being the only thing holding the business up.

Where our clients are

Adsvio works across the Haitian diaspora and the wider Caribbean: Florida, New York, Massachusetts, Quebec and Ontario, plus the Dominican Republic and Haiti itself. Pricing is set for the market you are actually in rather than converted down from a US rate.

Everything runs over video calls and WhatsApp, so what matters is whether the time zones line up. They usually do.

Questions

Questions we get asked.

Do you only work with Haitian businesses?

No. We work with small businesses of every background. The sample builds include a Montreal HR consultant and a Santo Domingo salon.

What is true is that we can do this in four languages and we understand the diaspora because we are part of it. Non-Haitian clients tend to read that as competence rather than exclusion, and they are right to.

Can you work with a business still in Haiti?

Yes, and we do. Payment, hosting and connectivity all need thinking about more carefully, and we will be straight with you on the call about what is realistic given the current situation.

What we will not do is take the money and then discover a problem in week three.

Will the site be in Kreyòl, English, or both?

Whatever serves your customers. Often the answer is both, with one leading: Kreyòl-first for a business whose customers are mostly first-generation, English-first with Kreyòl throughout where the audience is mixed.

We work that out in week one from who actually walks in, not from a preference.

Do you understand my kind of business?

Restaurants, salons, barbers, clinics, tax and immigration offices, shipping and barrel services, churches, event businesses, real estate. These are the businesses we come from.

If yours is something else, the 45-day process is the same. Book the call and we will tell you honestly whether we are the right people.
