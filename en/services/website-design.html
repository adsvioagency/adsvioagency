Website design

Small business website design that does one job well.

A website is not a brochure. It has one job: turn a stranger who found you into someone who contacts you. Most small business websites were never told what that job was.

What is actually wrong with most small business websites

It is almost never the design. Sit a business owner down and they will tell you their site looks fine. What they cannot tell you is what it is supposed to do, and that is the part that is broken.

We audit a lot of these before we build anything. The same three failures come up over and over.

1. It was built to look like a website

Somebody picked a template, dropped in a logo, wrote “Welcome to our website” and moved on. Every page exists because templates have those pages, not because a customer needed them. There is an About page nobody reads, a Services page that lists service names with no prices, and a Contact page with a form that goes to an inbox nobody checks.

A website that does its job is built backwards from one question: what does a stranger need to believe before they get in touch? Everything that helps them believe it stays. Everything else goes.

2. Nobody wrote it

Template copy is the most expensive thing on a cheap website. “We are passionate about delivering quality.” “Your success is our priority.” Sentences like that are not neutral. They actively signal that nobody is home. Your competitor down the road has the same sentence, which is exactly the problem.

Real copy is specific. It names what you do, who you do it for, what it costs, and what happens next. That is harder to write than it sounds, which is why so few small business sites have it.

3. It is slow, or it breaks on a phone

Most of your visitors are on a phone, often on mobile data, often standing somewhere with one bar. A site that takes six seconds to load has already lost most of them. So has a site where the phone number is not tappable, the map is a screenshot, and the menu button does nothing.

This is not a design problem either. It is a build problem, and it is entirely preventable: it just requires someone who cares about it.

What we do differently

We write before we design. The first week is spent on the audit and the strategy: who is actually buying, what they need to see, what your competitors are doing, and what the page has to say. Only then does anything get designed.

Then we build it properly. Hand-written HTML and CSS, no page builder, no forty plugins holding each other up. It loads fast because there is almost nothing to load. It works on a phone because it was designed on a phone first.

A site that loads in under two seconds and says one clear thing beats a beautiful site that says nothing, every time.

And it is connected to everything else on day one: your Google Business Profile, your social accounts, your booking, your WhatsApp. A website that sits on its own island is a business card. A website wired into where people actually find you is a system.

Questions

Questions about the website itself.

How many pages do I get?

Four on the Storefront Launch, five on the Authority Launch. That is genuinely enough for most small businesses. Extra pages usually dilute rather than add.

If you need more, they start at $100 each, and we will tell you on the call whether we think you actually need them.

Do I own the website?

Completely. The domain is in your name, the hosting account is yours, and you get every login on launch day.

There is no proprietary platform you are stuck on and no monthly fee required to keep the site online. If you ever want to move to another agency, you can, and nothing breaks.

Can I update it myself?

Yes, for the things that change: hours, prices, photos, a new service, a new post. We set that up and walk you through it on a call before handover.

Structural changes (a new page, a new section, a redesign) are quicker for us to do, and they are covered if you are on a monthly plan.

What if I already have a website?

We audit it first. Sometimes the right call is a rebuild. Sometimes it is fixing the three things holding it back, and you keep the rest.

If your current site is worth keeping we will say so, you pay less, and we spend the time on your profile and content instead. That is a worse outcome for our invoice and a better one for your business.
