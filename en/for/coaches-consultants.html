Who it’s for

When you are the product, the website has to do the convincing.

Nobody hires a consultant because of a website. But plenty of people decide not to call one because of a website, and you will never hear from them or find out why.

If this sounds like your year

You have been doing this for a decade and you are good at it. The work still comes, but it comes in waves — a referral, then a quiet month, then two at once. You know you should be publishing something, and you have started three times. Your site was built in 2019 by someone’s cousin and you have been meaning to deal with it ever since.

The referral well is not broken. It is just capped.

Almost every consultant we talk to has built their practice on referrals, and almost every one of them is quietly worried about it. Not because referrals stop — they rarely do — but because they arrive on their own schedule rather than yours.

Here is the part that gets missed. Your referrals are already checking you online before they call. Someone recommends you at a dinner, and the first thing that happens is your name goes into a search bar. What they find there either confirms the recommendation or quietly undercuts it.

So the website is not competing with word of mouth. It is finishing the job word of mouth started, and right now it may be undoing it.

What a stranger is actually deciding

They are not evaluating your methodology. In the ninety seconds they spend on your site they are answering three much blunter questions:

  • Is this person real? A face, a name, a specific history, somewhere they have actually worked.
  • Have they handled my exact problem? Not “strategy consulting” — the specific mess I am currently in.
  • What happens if I get in touch? Will I be sold to, or will I get a useful half hour?

A site that answers those three plainly beats a beautiful one that talks about passion and partnership. Most consultant websites answer none of them, because they were written to sound impressive rather than to be read by someone who is nervous.

The two-thirds who are not ready

Most people who land on your site have a problem they are not yet committed to solving. They are early. If the only thing you offer is “book a call”, you lose all of them, permanently, and you never know they were there.

This is what the lead magnet is for, and it is why it is in the package. One genuinely useful thing — a checklist, a template, a scripted conversation — that a nervous person can take without talking to you. Some of them come back in four months already convinced.

The second-best outcome is not “nothing”. It is an email address and permission to be useful again later.

Content, without it becoming a second job

You do not need to post daily and you do not need to be on video. What you need is enough published thinking that a sceptical stranger can see how you reason before they risk a call.

That is a much smaller number of pieces than the internet implies. A 90-day plan, twelve posts, and a rhythm you can actually keep. We build the plan and write the first quarter so the habit starts from something rather than from a blank page in January.

What changes

What is different 45 days later.

Not traffic. These are the changes that show up in your calendar.

Referrals close faster

The person who was recommended to you looks you up, finds a site that matches what they were told, and books instead of hesitating. This is the fastest-moving effect and usually the biggest.

You stop explaining yourself on calls

The part you are worried about

Four things consultants say on the first call.

“I get all my work from referrals, so I do not really need this.”

Then this is for the referrals. They are looking you up before they call — that is simply what people do now. The question is not whether a website brings you work, it is whether the one you have is quietly costing you some of the work you were already going to get.

“I do not want to look like I am selling.”

Good, because selling is not what works for consultants. Clarity works: what you do, who for, what it costs, what happens when someone gets in touch. That reads as confidence, not as a pitch. The pushy version is what happens when a site has nothing specific to say.

“I do not have time to produce content.”

You will not have to write it. We build the 90-day plan and write the first quarter from conversations with you — usually a couple of hours of your time in total. After that you decide whether to keep it running yourself or hand it to us monthly.

“My work is confidential. I cannot show client results.”

Common, and it is not the obstacle it looks like. Anonymised situations, your reasoning, the patterns you see repeatedly, the questions you ask — that demonstrates competence more convincingly than a logo wall. We build the proof section around what you can actually say.

Questions from consultants.

I am a solo practice. Is this overkill?

Solo practices are most of who we build this for. The Authority Launch is sized for one person who needs to look established without pretending to be an agency.

Nothing in it requires a team to maintain. That is deliberate — a foundation you cannot keep up with is worse than none.

Can you work in French?

Yes, and this comes up constantly with consultants in Montreal and Ottawa. We write in French, English, Spanish or Kreyòl, and bilingual sites are normal rather than an upgrade.

We translate as persuasion, not word for word. A literal translation of good sales copy is bad sales copy in the second language.

What if I do not know what my lead magnet should be?

Most people do not, and working it out is part of week one. It usually turns out to be something you already give away verbally — the thing you explain on every first call.

We write and design it. You do not hand us a finished PDF.

Do you do LinkedIn?

Yes, and for consultants it usually replaces Facebook in the package. Profile rebuilt properly, and content aimed there rather than at a feed your buyers are not in.

If your clients genuinely are on Instagram — and for some coaches they are — we go where they are instead.
