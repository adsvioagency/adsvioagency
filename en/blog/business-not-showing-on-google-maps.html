Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?

First establish whether the profile is missing entirely or simply not appearing for a service search. Those are different problems and need different checks.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 29 September 2026 3 min read

The short answer

Search for the exact business name and location, then review the profile dashboard for verification or policy notices. If the profile exists but does not appear for a broader query, investigate relevance and the search context. No single field change guarantees a position.

First, identify the problem

Check the exact business name and address or service area, then try a relevant service query. Record what you searched and where. A private browsing window does not remove every location or contextual influence.

Confirm that you are looking at the correct listing and signed into the account that manages it before changing anything.

1. Review verification and access

Look for a verification request or pending status in the management interface. Follow the requested steps using accurate evidence. Processing and available methods vary, so do not assume a fixed completion date.

2. Review the business description and categories

Check whether the profile accurately represents the main business and genuine services. Correct an inaccurate category, but do not add unrelated categories to chase searches. The profile guide explains the wider review.

3. Consider the search location

Local results depend partly on distance and relevance to the query. Seeing a different result from a customer in another area is not automatically evidence that the profile is broken. Keep service coverage accurate rather than inventing locations.

4. Check for a duplicate or old listing

Search for old business names and locations. If there appears to be a duplicate, review the appropriate ownership or duplicate-reporting process before deleting anything. An old listing can have information or history that needs careful handling.

5. Correct genuinely wrong details

Check that the phone number, website and location information identify the right business. Harmless differences such as “Ave.” and “Avenue” are not a reason to assume a ranking penalty. Focus on facts that could send customers to the wrong place.

6. Make the information useful

Review the services, current hours, permitted photos and contact links. A visitor should be able to understand whether the business meets their need. Filling a field is not a guarantee of greater visibility, but inaccurate information can frustrate a real customer.

7. Review feedback and policy notices

Genuine feedback helps people judge the business. Use a neutral review request process, not a target count intended to beat a competitor.

If the profile disappeared, check for a suspension or other notification and follow the indicated support process. Do not create a new listing to bypass it.

Work from evidence, then monitor

Record the problem, the correction and its date. Check that the change was accepted before evaluating later visibility. Compare similar queries and periods, with notes about the search location.

For help, start with Google Business Profile support or the wider local search foundation. Google explains the broad ranking factors in its official guidance.

Questions

Still wondering.

How quickly will a change appear?

It depends on the type of edit, verification and platform review. An accepted edit and an improvement in search visibility are different outcomes. There is no universal timeline.

Can I pay for a better organic map ranking?

Advertising and organic visibility are separate. Do not treat a paid placement as evidence that the underlying organic profile position improved.
