# SHOT LIST — photos of Pierre

All of it shootable on a phone, in window light, in about ninety minutes.
No studio, no lighting kit, no photographer.

---

## Before you start

**Light.** Stand facing a window, roughly two to three feet back, with the
window filling your field of view. Never with the window behind you — the
camera will expose for the bright glass and turn you into a silhouette.
Overcast days are the best light there is. Direct midday sun through glass is
the worst; if that is what you have, tape a white bedsheet over the window.

**Time of day.** Mid-morning or mid-afternoon. Avoid the hour around noon.

**Camera.** Rear camera, not the selfie camera — it is the better lens.
1× lens, never the wide one: it stretches faces at close range. Prop the
phone on a stack of books at chest height and use the volume button or a
3-second timer. Chest height, not held above you.

**Framing.** Leave generous room around yourself. The site crops these to
different shapes and a tight frame cannot be re-cropped. Shoot horizontal and
vertical of every setup — the site needs both.

**Settings.** Highest resolution, HEIC or JPEG. No filters, no Portrait mode
on the working shots (it blurs hands and objects unpredictably). Portrait
mode is fine on shot 1 only.

**Clothes.** Solid mid-tone colours. Deep green, warm white, charcoal, rust —
all of these sit well against this site's warm paper and teal. Avoid pure
white (blows out), pure black (goes flat), tight stripes and small checks
(they shimmer on screen), and visible logos. Bring two or three options and
change halfway through.

**Volume.** Shoot 20–30 frames of every setup. You will keep one. This is
normal and it is why professional shoots look effortless.

## The shots

### 1. The About page portrait — the most important frame
**Used on:** `/en/about` hero, next to "From Pétion-Ville to Santo Domingo"
**Orientation:** vertical, plus a horizontal safety frame

Head and shoulders to mid-chest. Face the window, body angled about 15° away
from the camera, head turned back to it. Eyes into the lens.

Not a grin. A settled, direct, slightly warm expression — the face of someone
about to say something true. Between frames, breathe out and drop your
shoulders; take the shot right after. Tension always shows in the jaw.

Background: a plain wall, two metres or more behind you so it falls soft.
Warm neutral is ideal.

### 2. Working at the desk — three-quarter
**Used on:** homepage proof section, About page
**Orientation:** horizontal

Seated at your actual desk, laptop open, mid-work. Look at the screen, not
the camera. Shoot from slightly off to one side so you read three-quarter,
not straight-on.

The desk should look used but not chaotic: laptop, notebook, one cup, maybe a
phone. Clear the cables and the clutter. What sells this frame is that it
looks like a Tuesday, not like a photo shoot.

### 3. On a call
**Used on:** `/en/contact`, and anywhere the free audit is offered
**Orientation:** horizontal and vertical

Mid-video-call: headphones or earbuds in, hand gesturing, speaking. Genuinely
talk while the timer runs — a mimed gesture always reads as mimed.

Do not show the client's face or name on your screen. Angle the laptop away
or use a blank call window.

### 4. Hands and screen — no face
**Used on:** service pages, guides, blog headers
**Orientation:** horizontal

Close on your hands at the keyboard, or holding a phone showing a client's
Instagram grid. Shot over your own shoulder or from the side.

These are the workhorses of the whole site. They fill space on long pages
without another photo of your face, and you can shoot ten variations in five
minutes. Get more of these than you think you need.

### 5. Notebook and planning
**Used on:** the ADSVIO Method sections, guides
**Orientation:** horizontal, shot from directly above

Looking straight down at an open notebook with real handwriting — a real
audit, a real week-one plan. Pen in frame, hand optional.

Write something real. Lorem ipsum and empty pages are visible from space.

### 6. Standing, arms crossed or hands in pockets
**Used on:** About page mid-section, founding-client block
**Orientation:** vertical

Full torso, standing, against a plain wall or in a doorway. Relaxed, weight
on one leg. Looking into the lens or just off it.

This is the authority frame. Keep your chin slightly forward and down — it
reads as considered rather than stiff.

### 7. Santo Domingo, environmental
**Used on:** About page, the `/en/haitian-business-marketing` page
**Orientation:** horizontal and vertical

You, outdoors, somewhere that is recognisably Santo Domingo — a colonial-zone
street, a colourful facade, a working street with real life in it. Small in
the frame; the place is the subject and you are in it.

Shoot in the last hour before sunset.

This is the one photo on the whole site that cannot be faked or bought, and
it is doing real work: the entire positioning rests on being Caribbean-founded
and actually there.

### 8. Two or three neutral verticals
**Used on:** wherever an unplanned gap appears
**Orientation:** vertical

You, plain background, no strong expression, generous space around you.
Boring on purpose. These are the ones that save a layout six months from now.

## Delivering them

- Full resolution, straight off the phone. Do not resize or compress —
  compressing twice is what makes web photos look muddy.
- No filters and no edits. Conversion to WebP with a JPG fallback, sizing and
  cropping all happen at build time.
- Name them by shot: `pierre-portrait-01.jpg`, `pierre-desk-03.jpg`.
- Drop them in `/assets/img/`.

**Minimum to unblock the About page:** shots 1, 2 and 6.
**Ideal:** all eight, 5–10 keepers total.

---

## One note on the wardrobe

The site's dark bands are deep teal `#0A5A4D` and the accent is a hot mango
orange. If you wear something in that green family for shot 1, the portrait
will look like it was art-directed for this site rather than dropped into it.
Rust or warm terracotta works for the same reason. It is a small thing that
makes the page look expensive.
