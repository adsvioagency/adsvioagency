Do you need a website if you have Instagram?

Instagram can help people discover your work. A website gives you another place to explain it and handle the next step. Whether you need one now depends on the buying decision.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 29 September 2026 4 min read

The short answer

You may not need a website immediately if your current audience, information and contact process already support the business. A website becomes useful when customers need clearer service details, a dependable destination or a more structured inquiry path. Keep social media if it is helping; the two can work together.

Where Instagram can help

Visual work, useful explanations and direct conversations can help people get to know a business. A restaurant can show its food, a contractor can explain a project and an agent can introduce a service. Those posts can support relationships even when the sale happens elsewhere.

The question is whether the right people understand the next step. Check your bio, current contact information and the destination of any booking link.

Where a profile may leave questions unanswered

Service details, pricing context and appointment steps can be difficult to find in a stream of posts. A website gives those answers stable pages and a structure you control. Some visitors also prefer to research without navigating a social account.

A website does not automatically get traffic. It still needs useful content, search accessibility and links from the places people already find you.

How to decide

  • What do people repeatedly ask before contacting you?
  • Can a new visitor find that answer without sending a message?
  • Does your contact path work reliably on a phone?
  • Do referrals have a clear place to check your services and background?
  • What would stop working if the social account were unavailable?

Use the answers to choose the next improvement rather than buying a website because every business is told to have one.

Choose the order around the gap

For an eligible local business, accurate Google profile information may be an early priority. For an online consultant, a clear service page may matter more. For a visual business already receiving inquiries, a better contact path may help before a full site.

There is no universal order. The online presence guide helps connect search, proof, social content and a website around the customer journey.

When it is reasonable to wait

You may be testing demand, operating at capacity or still deciding the service you want to offer. In those cases, keep information accurate and use a reliable way to receive inquiries while clarifying the business need.

Do not put off a broken booking path simply because a full website is not in budget. A focused correction may be the more useful next step.

How website and social work together

A useful post introduces a question or shows real work. The relevant service page explains the detail. A clear contact route lets the reader act. A genuine customer review can support the decision across those steps.

Link to the page that matches the post rather than sending everyone to a generic home page. Check the journey yourself and make sure the offer is consistent.

Three business examples

A visual local business: social images can build interest while a site holds service details and booking information. A home service team: project posts can lead to coverage and estimate information. A professional practice: educational content can lead to an explanation of services and the consultation process.

These are illustrative situations. Your own inquiries and customer questions should decide which content gets priority.

What a useful first website contains

Explain what you do, who it is for, where relevant services are available and how to contact you. Include accurate background and genuine proof where available. Use distinct pages when services need meaningful detail, not to hit a page-count target.

Compare scope with the website cost guide. A simple site can be appropriate without pretending every business needs the same four pages.

Keep the audience you already have

When you launch a site, update the profile link and explain what visitors can now find there. Keep useful posts and continue responding to relevant messages. The website should make the existing journey easier, not force loyal customers into an unfamiliar process.

Test links and forms before announcing them. If old pages are being replaced, map their useful destinations rather than allowing existing links to break.

Reduce dependence on one channel

Own your domain and keep access to your business accounts. Store approved original content and maintain more than one legitimate contact route. A website also needs maintenance and security; ownership does not mean nothing can go wrong.

Questions

Still wondering.

Can I keep using Instagram after launching a website?

Yes. Useful social content can lead visitors to service details or contact options on the website. Keep the information and next step consistent across both.

Will a website automatically bring customers?

No. It needs relevant content, accessibility and a clear contact journey, and people need a way to discover it. A website alone is not a guaranteed acquisition channel.

What should I fix first?

Start with the step where customers struggle: finding you, understanding the service, trusting the information or contacting you. The free Visibility Score reviews those parts together.
