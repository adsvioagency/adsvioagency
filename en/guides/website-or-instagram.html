Do you need a website if you have Instagram?

Most people asking this have been told yes by someone who sells websites. Here is the version that includes the cases where the answer is genuinely no.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 30 August 2026 7 min read

The short answer

If you sell to people who find you locally, you need a Google Business Profile more than either. If you sell expertise, you need a website. If you sell visual products to an audience you already have, Instagram alone can genuinely be enough for a while.

What Instagram is genuinely better at

This deserves saying plainly, because most articles on this question are written by people with a website to sell.

  • Being seen by people who were not looking for you. No website has a discovery feed. This is the single biggest thing Instagram does that a site cannot.
  • Showing work that is visual. Hair, food, nails, interiors, clothes. A grid is a better format for that than any gallery page.
  • Being current. A feed updated yesterday proves you are open. A website footer saying 2023 proves the opposite.
  • Being messaged. DMs have almost no friction. Contact forms have a lot.

If those four are your whole business, you can run on Instagram for a long time. Plenty of people do, profitably.

What Instagram cannot do

It does not appear when someone searches

Somebody two miles away opens their phone and types “braids near me”. They get a map with three businesses on it. Your Instagram is not in that result and cannot be.

This is the biggest gap, and note that closing it is not primarily a website job. It is a Google Business Profile job, and that is free.

You do not own it

A hacked account, a mistaken policy strike, a rule change. Businesses lose a decade of customer relationships this way, and there is often no route back.

Your website, your domain and your email list are the only assets in this list that cannot be taken away by a company that has never heard of you.

It cannot hold anything longer than a caption

Prices, a full service list, policies, the answer to a complicated question. People genuinely try to find these in a highlight reel and give up.

It looks the same as everyone else

Every profile has the same layout. When someone is choosing between you and two competitors, all three look equally credible, so the decision falls to follower count, which is the one metric that says least about whether you are good.

How to actually decide

Answer these honestly.

  1. Do people find you by searching, or by being shown you? Searching means you need the profile and probably the site.
  2. Is what you sell visual? If yes, Instagram carries more weight.
  3. Does a customer need to know a price, a policy or a process before buying? If yes, you need somewhere to put it.
  4. Are you the product? Coaches and consultants need a site. Credibility does not survive a grid.
  5. Would losing the account tomorrow end the business? If yes, you have a concentration risk, not a marketing preference.

The order to do it in

If you are starting from nothing and cannot do everything at once, this is the sequence that produces the most for the least.

OrderWhatCost
1Google Business Profile, filled in properlyFree
2A review system that actually asksFree
3Instagram or Facebook, whichever your customers useFree
4A four-page website you own$800+
5Content, consistentlyTime or $250/mo

Most businesses do this in exactly the wrong order: website first, profile never. The first two are free and beat the fourth for a local business.

The honest cases for “no”

You do not need a website right now if:

  • You sell something visual, your customers all come from the feed, and you are at capacity.
  • You are testing whether a business idea works at all. Prove demand first.
  • Your budget is under $500. Spend it on photography and the profile instead, and come back later.

In all three cases, still claim the Google Business Profile. It is free and takes an afternoon.

What the two do together

The framing of “versus” is the problem. They do different jobs in the same chain: Instagram is how people find and warm to you; the profile is how people searching find you; the site is where they decide and act.

Instagram gets you noticed. The profile gets you found. The website gets you hired.

The three businesses this question comes from

“Do I need a website” means something different depending on what you sell. Almost everyone asking is one of these three.

The visual product business

Clothes, cakes, nails, art, furniture. What you make photographs well and people buy with their eyes.

Verdict: Instagram carries you furthest, for longest. You still need a Google Business Profile if people can visit you, and you will eventually want somewhere to hold prices and shipping terms. But you can genuinely run for a year or two on the feed alone, and plenty do.

The local service business

Salon, restaurant, clinic, garage, shop. People find you because they are nearby and need something now.

Verdict: neither is your first priority. The map listing is. A complete Google Business Profile with real reviews beats both a website and an Instagram for this business, and it is free. Do that first, then a simple site to hold prices and answer questions, then social.

The expertise business

Consultant, coach, accountant, lawyer, therapist. People are buying your judgement, and the purchase is considered rather than impulsive.

Verdict: you need a website, and it is not close. Credibility does not survive a grid. A sceptical prospect wants to read how you think, see your credentials, and understand your process before they will book a call. None of that fits in a caption.

What a minimum viable website looks like

If the answer turned out to be yes, you need less than you think. Four pages covers almost every small business.

  • Home: what you do, who for, where, and one obvious next step above the fold.
  • Services: every service, described, with prices or at least a range.
  • About: who you are, with a real photo. This page converts far better than owners expect.
  • Contact: the way people actually reach you. For most of our clients that is WhatsApp, not a form.

That is it. A blog is optional and usually a distraction for a local business. Do not let anyone talk you into fourteen pages you will never update.

What that should cost is a separate question, and the honest range is narrower than most quotes suggest.

Moving without losing what you built

If you have run on Instagram for years, the worry is that a website means starting over. It does not, provided you do three things.

  1. Put the link in the bio and actually use it. Not a Linktree with nine options: one link, to the page that answers the question people are asking you in DMs.
  2. Move your FAQ off the highlights. Prices, hours, policies and process live on the site. Keep the highlights for proof and personality.
  3. Start collecting emails. This is the real reason to have a site. An email list is the only audience nobody can take away from you.

The concentration risk nobody talks about

Ask yourself plainly: if the account were gone tomorrow (hacked, mistakenly flagged, caught by a policy change), what would happen to the business on Monday?

If the answer is “it would end”, you do not have a marketing preference. You have a single point of failure, and every month you run without a backup is a month of accumulating risk against an asset you do not own and cannot appeal to.

That is not an argument for spending $3,000 tomorrow. It is an argument for owning something (a domain, four pages and a list of email addresses) before you need it.

Questions

Still wondering.

Can I just link my Instagram from Google?

You can put it as your profile's website link, and that is better than nothing. But it means every visitor lands on a feed rather than on an answer to the question that brought them, and you still cannot publish prices, policies or a service list anywhere durable.

What about Linktree instead of a website?

Fine as a temporary bridge, and much better than no link at all. It does not rank for anything, it is another platform you do not own, and it cannot hold real content. Treat it as a stopgap rather than a decision.

I have a Facebook page, not Instagram. Same answer?

Mostly, with one difference: Facebook pages do appear in Google results reasonably often, so a well-maintained page is a slightly better stopgap. Everything about ownership and depth still applies.
