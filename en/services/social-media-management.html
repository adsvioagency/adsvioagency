Social media management

Social media management for small business, without the guesswork.

You already know you should be posting. The problem was never motivation. It was that nobody told you what to post, and posting into silence gets old in about three weeks.

Why your last attempt stopped

Almost every small business owner we meet has tried this. They posted for a few weeks, got very little back, felt slightly foolish, and stopped. Then they felt guilty about stopping, which is somehow worse.

None of that is a discipline problem. Here is what actually happened.

You were posting without a plan

Deciding what to post on the day you post it is the hardest possible version of this job. You are inventing strategy, writing copy and making a design in the twenty minutes before you open. Of course it is exhausting, and of course it stops.

With a content plan the decision is already made. You are not asking “what should I post?” You are executing something that was decided calmly, three weeks ago, by someone who had thought about it.

You were posting the wrong things

Most small business feeds are announcements. New hours. A promotion. A holiday greeting. These are the posts that perform worst, because nobody follows a business to receive announcements.

What works is the stuff you find boring because you see it every day: how the thing is made, why you do it that way, the customer who came in with an unusual problem, the difference between the cheap version and yours. That is not marketing content, it is just your job, described.

You were measuring the wrong thing

Likes are not the point and never were. A salon with 400 engaged local followers will out-earn one with 40,000 scattered ones, every single time.

The number that matters is whether people arrive already knowing who you are. That shows up in your DMs and at your counter, not in your analytics tab.

How we run it

Every month starts with a plan you can see: what is going out, on what day, on which platform, and why. You approve it in one sitting. Then we write, design and publish it, and you get on with running your business.

We shoot nothing ourselves. We work from what you have plus what we ask you to send. A voice note from your car about a job you just finished is worth more than a stock photo, and it takes you ninety seconds.

Consistency beats brilliance. Twelve decent posts a month beats three excellent ones and a five-week gap.

And it runs in whichever language your customers think in. If half your audience is Haitian and half is American, we post in both rather than picking one and quietly losing the other half.

Questions

Questions about running social.

Which platforms do you cover?

Instagram and Facebook as standard, because that is where almost every small business audience actually is. LinkedIn instead of Facebook for consultants and B2B.

We would rather do two platforms properly than five badly. If TikTok genuinely matters for your business we will say so, and if it does not we will say that too.

Do I have to be on camera?

No. Plenty of businesses we work with never appear on camera and do fine: product, process, results and text-led posts carry it.

That said, if you are a coach or consultant, you are the product, and a face does outperform a logo. We will push you gently and then drop it.

Who owns the accounts?

You do, always. We work inside your accounts using proper delegated access, never by holding your password.

If we stop working together you change one setting and everything (the followers, the posts, the history) stays exactly where it is.

What do you need from me each month?

About thirty minutes. One approval pass on the plan, and photos or voice notes when we ask for something only you can provide.

The whole point is that the thinking, the writing and the designing stop being your job. If it is taking you more than an hour a month, we have built it wrong.
