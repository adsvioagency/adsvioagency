Guide

The complete Google Business Profile guide for local businesses

Work through the profile as a customer would: correct business, clear services, useful information and a working contact route. Check eligibility and ownership before adding or changing a listing.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 29 September 2026 5 min read

The short answer

Begin with eligibility and access, then review the real business details, relevant categories and customer contact links. Use genuine photos and a neutral review request process. A complete profile can help explain your business, but does not guarantee a search position.

Where a business profile fits

A Business Profile can show information about an eligible business in Google Search and Maps. It complements a website rather than replacing every service page. A customer may use the profile to find a number and the site to understand the work.

Start with Google's eligibility and representation guidance. Online-only businesses should not invent an address to obtain a listing.

How to think about local visibility

Google describes local ranking in terms of relevance, distance and prominence. There is no fixed category or review-count formula that guarantees first place. See Google's local ranking explanation.

Compare meaningful searches from the customer's context. Finding the exact business name and appearing for a broad service query are different questions.

Step one: access and verification

Search for the existing business before creating another profile. If one exists, check who manages it and use the ownership process where needed. Keep the business owner in control of the account.

Follow the verification method and evidence requested by Google for that profile. Availability and processing times vary. Do not promise a launch date for a step controlled by the platform.

Step two: accurate categories

Choose a primary category that describes the actual core business. Add relevant categories only when they describe what the business really does. Competitor research can suggest questions to investigate, but should not replace accuracy.

Keep a record of changes and the reason for them. Avoid repeated speculative changes when there is no clear evidence that the current category is wrong.

Step three: customer-facing details

Review the name, contact number, hours and appropriate address or service-area setup. Check the website destination and whether customers can reach the team through it. Correct genuinely outdated details rather than worrying about harmless punctuation differences.

For businesses that travel to customers, make the public information reflect that model. An appointment-only practice and a walk-in shop should not present the same visit instructions.

Step four: services and available fields

Use the fields available for your category to explain actual services. Keep descriptions clear and current. If you publish prices, explain the scope and avoid a number that could mislead someone about what is included.

A contractor might distinguish installation and repair; an accountant might separate bookkeeping and tax preparation. Use the business's actual terminology and capabilities.

Step five: real photographs

Use material that helps a customer recognize the business and understand its work. Select accurate images of the team, premises or completed work where permission allows. Avoid stock material that implies it depicts your actual location or projects.

Keep copies and permissions in an organized folder. Replace outdated images when the business changes rather than uploading for the sake of a numerical target.

Step six: a neutral review process

Make it easy for customers to share genuine feedback. A clear request and accessible link help them understand what to do. Do not promise a rating or steer the wording toward a desired outcome.

Use our review request guide for the message, timing and follow-up considerations.

Step seven: maintain what changes

Assign someone to review hours, service changes, contact links and important notifications. A profile is a live business record, not a task that is permanently complete after launch.

Keep the same changes reflected on the website where relevant. Distinct call and messaging numbers can be useful when each channel is accurately identified.

What to expect after changes

Publication, verification and search visibility are separate steps. Check whether the edit was accepted before deciding whether it helped. There is no universal number of days for a change to produce more inquiries.

If the profile is missing, work through the troubleshooting checklist. Record the change date so future comparisons have context.

Posts, offers and events

Use available posting features for current, useful business updates. Check dates, terms and the destination link before publication. A post should help someone understand or act, not repeat the same search phrase.

Feature availability can vary. Use the current dashboard and guidance rather than assuming every profile supports the same options.

Contact and booking links

Test every contact or booking option you publish. Confirm the destination, mobile behavior and who handles the inquiry. Do not assume a click means an appointment was completed.

If a messaging option is available, use it only when the business can manage responses. Keep sensitive personal information out of ordinary public interactions.

Read the available performance data

Look for the searches and customer actions available in your profile reporting. Compare equivalent periods and note changes in opening hours, services or campaigns. A reported interaction is not automatically a new customer.

Combine those signals with actual inquiry quality and your website data where available. Avoid attributing every change to the last field you edited.

If a profile is suspended

Read the notification and review the underlying business information before submitting an appeal. Gather the evidence requested and follow the current support process. Do not create another profile to bypass the problem.

A suspension is different from ranking lower for a service query. Identify which problem you have before choosing a remedy.

A manageable monthly review

Check current details, notifications, customer questions and contact links. Review feedback thoughtfully and add accurate new information when the business changes. Give the task a named owner.

Questions

Still wondering.

Can an online-only business create a profile?

No. Check the current Google eligibility rules before creating a listing. Do not use a borrowed or fictional address to make an online-only business appear local.

Does completing every field guarantee a better ranking?

No. Complete accurate information helps customers understand the business, but placement depends on multiple factors and the search context.

Can Adsvio verify the profile for me?

We can guide the steps and prepare accurate information within the agreed scope. Google may require the owner to provide evidence or take action, and approval remains Google's decision.
