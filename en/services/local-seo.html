Local SEO

Local SEO for small business, without the monthly mystery.

Local SEO has a bad reputation, and it earned it — years of agencies charging $1,000 a month for a report nobody reads. The underlying work is neither mysterious nor endless.

What local SEO actually is

Stripped of the jargon: making it easy for Google to be confident about who you are, where you are, and what you do — and making it obvious to a human that you are the right choice once they find you.

That is genuinely most of it. There is no secret, and for a local business there is not very much of it either. Which is precisely why the monthly-retainer model is so uncomfortable: once the work is done, it is largely done.

The profile is most of the game

For local searches, your Google Business Profile outweighs your website. Categories, completeness, photos and reviews decide whether you appear in the map pack at all. We covered that in detail on the Google Business Profile page, and it is the first thing we fix.

Consistency across listings

Your name, address and phone number need to match everywhere they appear — your site, your profile, Facebook, Apple Maps, directories. When they disagree, Google gets less confident, and less confidence means lower placement.

This is dull, mechanical work. It is also the thing most often left undone, usually because a business moved, changed a phone number, or was listed by someone else years ago and nobody noticed.

A site Google can read

Fast, mobile-first, with a real page for each service rather than one page listing them all. Titles and descriptions written for what people actually type. Structured data so your hours, location and services can be read without guessing. Your town in your copy, because you are competing locally and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

Reviews, which never stop mattering

Volume, recency, rating, and whether you reply. This is the one part that keeps working after the build, and it is the part we hand you a system for rather than charge you monthly to operate.

What we will not do

We do not buy links, and we will not build a network of fake directory listings. Both still get sold, both are risky, and neither is worth what it costs you when it goes wrong.

If someone guarantees you a number-one ranking, they are either lying or about to do something to your website that you would not agree to if they explained it.

And we will not sell you a monthly local SEO retainer for work that is finished. If the useful work is done, we would rather move that budget to content or ads, where it keeps earning.

Questions

Questions about local search.

How long does it take to work?

Profile changes often show within days. Site and listing work takes four to eight weeks to settle. Reviews compound over a quarter and keep going.

Anyone giving you a date is guessing. What we can tell you honestly is the order things usually move in.

Do I need to blog to rank locally?

Usually not. For most local businesses the profile, the service pages and the reviews do nearly all of the work, and a blog is a distraction from that.

Content earns its place when you are competing on expertise rather than proximity — which is the consultant case, not the salon case.

What about my competitor who is always first?

Check whether they are simply closer to the person searching. Distance is a ranking factor and it moves with the searcher, so the map you see is not the map your customers see.

Where they are genuinely outranking you, it is nearly always reviews or a more complete profile. Both are catchable.

Can you do this if I am not in the US or the DR?

Yes. The mechanics are the same everywhere Google operates, and we work across the US, Canada, the Dominican Republic and the wider Caribbean.

What changes is the language people search in, which is exactly the thing we are set up for.
