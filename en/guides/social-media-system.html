The 4-hour-a-month social media system

A monthly routine for deciding what to post before the week gets busy. Use four hours as a starting planning budget, then adjust for your formats, approvals and production needs.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 29 September 2026 5 min read

The short answer

Choose an audience and a manageable platform mix, plan useful topics together, then produce and review the posts in batches. Four hours is an example budget for a simple routine, not a promise that filming, editing and replies will fit into it.

Why posting becomes difficult

Deciding the topic, writing a caption and finding an image each time creates repeated work. Separate those decisions. Keep one list of customer questions and one folder of approved material, then use a scheduled planning session to turn them into posts.

Start with the service you want people to understand. A tax preparer might explain what to bring to an appointment. A contractor might explain an estimate. A consultant might describe who a particular engagement is for.

The monthly planning session

Set aside two hours as an initial experiment: 20 minutes to review the previous month, 40 minutes to choose topics and an hour to draft captions. If that is not enough, reduce the number of posts or allow more time. Do not remove accuracy checks to hit a time target.

  • Review relevant questions, messages and website visits, not only likes.
  • Choose one audience and the service or decision each post supports.
  • Assign each draft an owner, an image or format and an approval date.
  • Check the destination link before scheduling.

Five useful content types

These categories are a starting framework, not a rule for every account.

  • Proof: approved real work, with context and permission.
  • Teach: a clear explanation of something customers find confusing.
  • Process: what happens before, during or after the work.
  • Answer: a question that repeatedly comes up in inquiries.
  • Invite: the service, who it suits and how to take the next step.

For law, accounting and healthcare content, the responsible professional should review technical wording. Use general process explanations where a short post cannot safely answer a personal question.

Production and publishing across the month

Use the remaining two hours of the example budget to organize existing images, format simple posts and schedule them. Filming, detailed design, translation and moderation may require additional time. A realistic schedule is more useful than a full calendar nobody can maintain.

Keep originals in your own folder. Record permissions and who approved the caption. Check the post after publication and make time for questions that require an actual response.

Measure attention and the next step

Use reach and saves to understand what people notice. Use relevant messages, destination-page visits and qualified inquiries to understand what happens next. Ask new inquiries how they heard about you when that information is missing.

A post with limited reach can still answer a valuable customer question. A popular post may attract people outside your market. Review both relevance and response before changing the plan.

Choose platforms around the audience

Instagram can suit visual work and short explanations. Facebook can support an existing local community. LinkedIn may be useful for professional and business-to-business relationships. Start with where your actual customers engage and what your team can produce consistently.

A realtor showing approved listing material has a different content job from an accountant explaining a consultation process. Use the professional-services and home-services examples to frame the decision, then test it with your own audience.

If customers use more than one language

Choose the language of a post deliberately. Important service and contact information may need more than one version; a daily update may not. Keep the meaning consistent and have someone fluent review the wording.

Do not assume a bilingual audience splits evenly. Look at the language of real questions and whether your team can respond in the language you publish.

An example month for a contractor

This is an illustrative plan, not an Adsvio client campaign: explain the estimate process, show a permitted finished project, answer a preparation question, and invite a relevant estimate request. Repeat the useful types with different real material across the month.

If twelve posts are manageable, one starting mix is four work examples, three explanations, two process posts, two questions and one invitation. If four posts are realistic, start there. No ratio guarantees inquiries.

Write a caption around one question

Lead with the useful point. Then explain the example and give a next step only when it fits. For example: “Planning a kitchen update? Before an estimate, send the room dimensions and the changes you want to discuss.” The actual request must match the contractor's process.

Avoid unsupported promises, invented testimonials and claims that every reader has the same problem. The caption should sound like something the business can stand behind in a conversation.

Choose formats you can sustain

A simple photograph may be enough to explain a job. A carousel can walk through steps. A short video can demonstrate a process. Choose the format that helps the audience understand, then review the response instead of assuming one format always performs best.

Add useful descriptions or captions where appropriate. Reuse a strong explanation across channels with adjustments for the audience rather than publishing an identical block everywhere.

When to delegate the work

Consider help when useful content is repeatedly delayed or the work consumes time you need for delivery. Compare the cost with the actual scope: planning, writing, design, scheduling, approvals and response coverage are different tasks.

Questions

Still wondering.

Will this take only four hours?

Four hours is an example budget for a simple monthly routine using material you already have. Video production, translation, approvals and replies can require more time. Adjust the output to the time available.

How long until social media generates inquiries?

There is no fixed timeline. Review whether the content reaches a relevant audience and whether the contact path works. Track qualified inquiries alongside engagement rather than treating a posting streak as proof of return.

Do I need to make videos?

No. Start with formats you can create accurately and consistently. Video is useful when it demonstrates something better than a photograph or written explanation.
