Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?

This is the question we get asked most, and it is almost always one of seven things. Here they are in the order worth checking, from the ones you can fix today to the ones you cannot fix at all.

By Pierre Runald Février Updated 30 August 2026 3 min read

First, check it properly

Before diagnosing anything: search your business name on Google Maps while logged out, or in a private window. Your own account’s history makes you see results nobody else sees, which is why owners often think everything is fine.

Then search the thing a customer would type: “barber near me”, not your business name. Those are completely different searches and the second one is the one that matters.

1. The profile is unverified

An unverified profile can exist and still not appear in results. Check the dashboard for a verification prompt. Video verification is now common: one continuous clip showing your signage, your equipment and that you have access to the premises.

Fixable in a few days, sometimes a couple of weeks. Free.

2. Your categories are wrong or missing

This is the most common real cause. Your primary category decides which searches you can appear in at all. A salon that also does nails and lashes but has only picked “Hair salon” is invisible for two-thirds of its own services.

Set the most specific accurate primary category, then add secondary ones for everything else you genuinely do. This one change moves more than anything else on the list.

The full detail is in the Google Business Profile guide.

3. You are simply further away

Distance from the searcher is a ranking factor, and it is the one nobody controls. A competitor two blocks closer will beat you for some searches no matter what you do.

This is worth accepting early. The winnable fight is the comparison where you are genuinely the better choice and are losing anyway, which is usually reviews.

4. There is a duplicate listing

A business that moved, changed its name, or was listed years ago by a supplier often has two profiles splitting its signals. Search your address as well as your name. Report the extra one as a duplicate.

5. Your information disagrees with itself

Your name, address and phone number need to match everywhere: profile, website, Facebook, directories. When they disagree, Google gets less confident, and less confidence means lower placement.

Character for character. “Ave.” on one and “Avenue” on another is enough to count as a mismatch.

6. The profile is half empty

Google measures completeness. No services, no description, four photos from 2019 and no attributes reads as a business that may not be operating.

Filling everything in is an afternoon of work and it is free. Most of your competitors have not done it.

7. You have almost no reviews

Volume, recency and rating all feed prominence. Two reviews from 2022 will lose to eleven from this year, even if yours are five stars.

Most businesses have no system for asking, which is why this stays broken for years. Fixing it is the highest-return thing available after categories.

What about a suspension?

If your profile vanished entirely rather than ranking badly, check your email for a suspension notice. The usual causes are keywords stuffed into the business name, a virtual office used as an address, or a category that does not match what you do. Appeals take two to four weeks.

The order to work through

Verification, then categories, then duplicates, then completeness, then reviews. The first four are an afternoon. The last one is a quarter, and it is the one that keeps paying afterwards.

Questions

Still wondering.

How long until changes show up?

Completeness and photo changes often appear within days. Category changes take two to six weeks to settle. Review-driven movement is a matter of months, and it compounds rather than jumping.

Can I pay Google to rank higher on the map?

No. Local Services Ads and Google Ads can put you above the map results as an ad, but they do not change your organic map ranking. Anyone offering to pay Google for placement is describing something that does not exist.
