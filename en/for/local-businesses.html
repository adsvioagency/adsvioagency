Who it’s for

Someone three blocks away is getting your customer.

They are not better than you. Their food is not better, their work is not better. They just show up first on the map, and when they do they look like a business worth trusting.

If this sounds like your week

The business runs on regulars and word of mouth, and it works — most days. But you know the new place that opened last year is busier than it should be, and you know a lot of people who should have found you never did. You have been meaning to sort the website out for two years. You are also working ten hours a day, so it stays on the list.

How people actually choose a local business

Somebody two miles away pulls out a phone and searches. What they get is a map with three businesses on it, each with a star rating, a photo, opening hours and a Call button.

They pick from that map. Most of them never visit anybody’s website at all. The whole decision takes about fifteen seconds, and it is made on four things:

  • Are you even on the map? If your profile is unclaimed or badly categorized, you are not in the running.
  • What does the rating look like? Not just the number — how many, and how recent.
  • Do the photos look like a real, currently-open place? Four dark photos from 2019 read as closed.
  • Can I do the thing right now? Call, book, order, get directions. One tap.

Every one of those is fixable in a month, and none of them requires you to be better at your actual job. That is the frustrating part and also the good news.

The website still matters, just not the way you think

For a local business the site is not the front door any more — the map listing is. But the site is what people check when the decision is bigger than a coffee: a clinic, a law office, a salon they are trusting with their hair before a wedding.

For those, the site answers the questions the map cannot. What exactly do you do. What does it cost. Who works there. What happens when I arrive. A page that answers those plainly, and loads in a second on mobile data, closes people the listing warmed up.

Reviews are the part that compounds

Most businesses get reviews by accident, which means they mostly get them from people who were annoyed. The happy customer — the one who came back three times — never thinks to leave one, because nobody ever asked.

A review system is not complicated. It is a message, sent at the right moment, with a link that takes one tap. A QR code on the counter and on the receipt. That is the whole thing, and it is the single highest-return piece of what we build.

Ten recent reviews will do more for you than a redesign. We build the system that gets them, then hand it to you so it keeps running without us.

And it should reach people in their language

If half your customers are Haitian, Dominican or Cuban, an English-only presence is quietly telling them the place is not really for them. That is not a translation problem, it is a customer problem, and it is one of the more common ones we fix.

Concrete changes, in the order they usually show up.

You appear where you did not

A properly categorized, complete profile starts showing for searches you were invisible for. This often moves within days, and it is the fastest win available.

Reviews start arriving on purpose

The part you are worried about

Four things owners say on the first call.

“I am already busy. I do not need more customers.”

Fair, and worth taking seriously. But busy is not the same as full, and it is not the same as protected. The place that opened last year is going to keep growing, and the customers it takes are the ones who never knew you existed.

The other thing this fixes is the type of customer. Published prices and clear services filter out the enquiries that waste your time before they reach you.

“All my customers come by word of mouth.”

Then the same thing applies as for consultants: the person who was told about you is looking you up before they walk in. What they find either confirms the recommendation or quietly weakens it.

Word of mouth also has a ceiling — it only reaches as far as your existing customers’ circles. The map reaches everyone within two miles.

“I do not have time for this.”

Two hours a week, across the 45 days. One kickoff call, one short review each week, and answering questions when we have them.

We write the copy, build the site, set up the profile and produce the first month of content. If it is taking you more than that, we have built it wrong.

“I tried an agency before and they disappeared.”

We hear this more than anything else, and it is why the whole offer is shaped the way it is. Fixed price agreed before we start, a fixed 45 days, and something to look at every single week.

If we miss that date for any reason on our end, we keep working at no additional cost until it is live. That is the only answer to this objection that means anything.

Questions

Questions from local businesses.

I do not have a website at all. Where do we start?

That is a cleaner starting point than a bad website, honestly. Nothing to undo and no rankings to protect.

We start with the Google Business Profile in week one because it moves fastest, and the site is built alongside it.

Can customers book or order through it?

Yes. Reservations, appointments or orders, connected to whatever system you already use — and if you do not use one, WhatsApp click-to-chat, which for most of our clients converts better than a booking engine.

We test it on a real phone before launch rather than assuming it works.

What if I need it in Spanish or Kreyòl?

That is normal here rather than an add-on to the build. We write the site in whichever language your customers think in, and in two if your customers are split.

The Atelier Nord sample build is Spanish-first for exactly this reason.

Do I have to keep paying you every month?

No. The 45-day build is a one-time project and everything is yours at the end — domain, hosting, logins, content.

Most clients ask us to keep running social monthly afterwards. That is optional, month to month, and we talk about it after launch rather than before.
